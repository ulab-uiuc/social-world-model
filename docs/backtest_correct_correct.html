SWM news-driven backtest — with real hourly fills

The definitive version of the news-driven backtest. Same idea — walk the jin10 wire, trade the markets each headline is about — but now priced against the real hourly polymarket order book (polymarket_2026_series.jsonl): a trade fills at the next hourly tick after the headline (median lag ~0.5h) and holds 24h, instead of snapping to a once-a-day reconstructed quote.

The verdict survives the more honest fill: a small, statistically real directional edge that the bid-ask spread very nearly — but not quite — cancels. Over 2,067 out-of-sample headlines the strategy opens 817 positions and returns +1.7% on capital (95% CI −5.4% to +9.3%), against −5.3% for blind YES and −2.9% for a coin flip on the same universe. Frictionless it earns +8% to +14% and rises with confidence; the 1c spread eats almost all of it.

What changed from the earlier writeups, and why. The first version filled at the last quote before the headline — you captured the news move for free (an unrealistic +1.0% that flipped to −6% under any slippage). This version fills at the first hourly price after the news, so the quote has already absorbed it. That is the honest "react to the headline" trade, and it is only possible because the hourly book actually covers the test window.

1. Design

One bi-encoder, two directions: reverse retrieval picks which markets a headline may trade; forward retrieval fills each prompt with that market's recent news so the model input matches training. The world model, P&L accounting and baselines are the same code as the grid backtest. Two price sources are kept strictly apart: the daily history the model was trained on goes into the prompt; the hourly book is used only to fill and settle the trade. Nothing published after the headline ever reaches the model.

2. Universe and how a trade is decided

2,067 out-of-sample headlines (2026-06-17 onward) → 2,175 scored cells → 1,733 distinct positions → 817 trades, across 184 markets that have an hourly book. The set is balanced: 37.7% up, mean move −0.2c, mean entry 0.265 — so buying YES blind loses, and a positive return must come from direction.

The 817 falls out of three filters (model and baselines alike): dedup a daily-quoted position → keep only |pred − entry| ≥ 0.05 → drop unaffordable fills. Baselines ignore the signal and trade all 1,733.

4. Real signal, eaten by the spread

Set the spread to zero and the edge is unmistakable and rises with confidence; put 1c back and it nearly vanishes:

Gross edge is ~0.7–1.1c/share; the spread is 1c. The forecast correlates +0.084 with the tradeable move (direction accuracy 51.1%) — real, thin. What survives is fragile: the +1.7% leans on ~54 trades in the 0.75–0.98 price bucket (+14%) and flips between June (−2.4%) and July (+7.2%). And it still assumes you get that next-hour price: model a further 25% of slippage and it falls to −5.2%.

Three it got wrong

jin10 · Iran Foreign Ministry: Switzerland meeting postponed, rescheduling underway
Will the next US–Iran meeting be in Switzerland? BOUGHT NO
Read a scary word too literally. "Postponed" ≠ cancelled — the meeting still happened in Switzerland. Shorted to 0.11; settled YES. −56c / share
jin10 · Market sources: Iran seeks guarantees of an end to hostilities before resuming talks
Will Iran agree to end uranium enrichment by July 31? BOUGHT YES
Too optimistic on a diplomacy headline. "Seeks guarantees" is a precondition, not a deal. Bought 0.63; it collapsed to 0.14. −50c / share
jin10 · SpaceX (SPCX.O) launches inaugural senior unsecured bond offering to repay debt
SpaceX IPO: closing price up at end of first month? BOUGHT YES
Misjudged a mixed corporate signal. A debt-repay bond issue read as bullish; the stock fell. Bought 0.81; settled 0.46. −37c / share

Winners and losers together are the whole point of §4: the model calls direction right a little more than half the time, so the gross edge is positive — but the losses are big and frequent enough that, after the spread, the net is close to a coin toss. Real signal, no moat.

6. Reproduce

# build: fill/settle on the hourly book, prompt on the daily history
python scripts/backtest_build_news_grid.py \
    --data data/swmbench_jin10_dailyhist_en.jsonl \
    --price-series data/polymarket_2026_series.jsonl \
    --out results/backtest/grid_news_hourly.jsonl \
    --embed-model <bge-small-en-v1.5> --top-markets 5 --hold-hours 24
python scripts/backtest_predict.py --grid results/backtest/grid_news_hourly.jsonl \
    --model-path <checkpoint> --out results/backtest/preds_news_hourly.jsonl
python scripts/backtest_report.py --preds news_hourly=results/backtest/preds_news_hourly.jsonl \
    --out results/backtest/report_news_hourly.json

Model under test: world_model_qwen2p5_3b_jin10d_bal (a Qwen2.5-7B world model, the dir name's "3b" is a leftover). Knobs that move the result most: --top-markets, --hold-hours, and in the report --threshold, --cost, --entry-drift.

7. Bottom line

Priced against the real hourly book — filling at the price a trader could actually get, an hour after the news — the world model shows the same thing the grid backtest did: a genuine directional signal (frictionless +8–14%, rising with confidence, direction 51%) that the bid-ask spread nearly cancels (+1.7% on capital, a confidence interval straddling zero). It has learned which markets a headline moves and in which direction. That skill is real; it is not yet worth more than it costs to trade.
