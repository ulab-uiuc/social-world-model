SWM news-driven backtest

The grid backtest run from the opposite axis: walk the jin10 wire in time order, and for each important headline retrieve the markets it is about and trade them. Model under test: world_model_qwen2p5_3b_jin10d_bal — a Qwen2.5-7B world model (the dir name's "3b" is a leftover) trained on the debiased daily jin10 set.

Same verdict, opposite direction: a small, statistically real directional edge worth about as much as the bid-ask spread — no more.

1. Design

One bi-encoder, two directions: reverse retrieval picks which markets a headline may trade; forward retrieval fills each prompt with that market's recent news so the input matches training. Everything after cell-building — the 7B model, the P&L, the baselines — is the grid backtest's code unchanged; only scripts/backtest_build_news_grid.py is new. Every input is anchored at the headline time (no look-ahead); the settle quote is a P&L label only.

2. Universe and how a trade is decided

2,067 out-of-sample headlines (2026-06-17 onward) → 2,812 scored cells → 1,297 distinct positions → 589 trades, across 183 markets. The set is balanced: 38% up, mean move -0.2c, mean entry 0.271 — so buying YES blind loses, and a positive return must come from calling direction.

The 589 falls out of three filters (model and baselines alike): dedup a daily-quoted market to one position → keep only |pred − entry| ≥ 0.05 → drop unaffordable fills. Baselines ignore the signal and trade all 1,297. (Entry and the 24h anchor coincide here, so momentum/contrarian fire on zero cells and SWM's edge is pure forecast, not mean-reversion.)

4. Real signal, eaten by the spread

Set the spread to zero and the edge is unmistakable and monotone in confidence; put 1c back and it collapses:

Gross edge is ~0.8–1.2c/share; the spread is 1c. The regression agrees the signal is real — forecast vs. tradeable move correlates +0.086, t = +3.1 over 1,297 positions, direction accuracy 51.6% — but on capital after costs the strategy is indistinguishable from flat (t = 0.38). What little survives is fragile: the +1.0% leans on ~35 trades in the 0.75–0.98 price bucket (+30%) and flips between June (-2.1%) and July (+6.1%).

Will Silver hit $60 (LOW) by end of June? BOUGHT YES
Silver selling off → more likely to touch the low. Model raised YES (0.48→0.56); it hit. +51.0c / share
jin10 · Pre-market: SpaceX (SPCX.O) down over 5%, US AI-related stocks mixed
SpaceX IPO: closing price up at end of first month? BOUGHT NO
Shares down 5% pre-market → "closes up" less likely. Model shaded NO (0.85→0.79); price fell. +42.5c / share

This is the machinery behind the frictionless +8–16%: on the trades it takes, the forecast leans the right way often enough to capture a real slice of the move. Two honesty notes the full paths make visible: the model's own edge is small (it nudged JD Vance only 0.38→0.46) — most of the realised P&L is the market then moving a lot in that direction; and entries are read off a daily-sampled series, so some are dips (JD Vance) or a few hours stale (SpaceX). And the catch from §4 still holds — average this edge across all trades, subtract the 1c spread, and the typical trade clears little more than costs. These are the tail where it worked, not the mean.

Bottom line: from the news side, the same story as the grid — the model has learned which markets a headline moves and in which direction (t = +3.1, +8–16% frictionless), and that skill is not yet worth more than it costs to trade (+1.0%, CI straddling zero).
